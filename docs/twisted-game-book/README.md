# The Twisted Game Book
## Family & Friends Rival Day III — working master

This folder is the home for the event's **Twisted Game Book**, also called the **Game Day Bible**. It is an operating manual, not just a list of funny game names.

The existing project conversation and repository canon say this work follows the Rival Reaper delivery; it was not cancelled. Preserve earlier work where it exists, and add recovered material without pretending unverified concepts were approved.

## The personality

**Field Day got a mouth on it.** This is organized chaos with a whistle around its neck: a family cookout collided with a fantasy sports league, hood Olympics and a championship tournament. The writing should be funny, loud, competitive, urban and unorthodox. The rules themselves must still be short, fair and clear enough for a player to understand on the first read.

The event exists to bring family and friends together. Rivalry is temporary; family is the point.

## Official game status labels

Use these labels consistently:
- **RECOVERED — OWNER REPORTED:** information directly recorded from the owner's prior instructions, not yet physically rechecked.
- **PROPOSED:** a concept for discussion; not approved for play.
- **DRAFT FOR APPROVAL:** full rules exist but the owner has not approved them.
- **APPROVED:** the owner explicitly approved the final rules.
- **RETIRED:** no longer in use.
- **NEEDS EQUIPMENT CHECK:** the game concept is clear but one or more required items have not been confirmed.

Do not turn a funny idea into an approved game without the owner's final approval.

## For every game, record

1. Game name and status.
2. The plain-English explanation.
3. What makes it twisted/unorthodox.
4. Number of players and team participation.
5. Equipment and quantities.
6. Setup.
7. How to play, step by step.
8. How to win and scoring.
9. Penalties/disqualifications.
10. Tie-breaker.
11. Referee duties and what the audience should know.
12. Safety and accessibility notes.
13. What is already owned, what can be borrowed, what must be purchased, and optional upgrades.
14. Owner approval date/version.

## Approval-to-purchase workflow

1. Recover the equipment we already own.
2. Pitch and develop the game concept in the project's voice.
3. Write the complete rules in plain English.
4. Identify team/player count, setup, scoring, penalties, tie-breakers and safety.
5. Make an itemized equipment list.
6. Separate **OWNED / BORROW / BUY / OPTIONAL**.
7. Present the complete game for the owner to approve.
8. Only after approval, mark it APPROVED and add its approved items to the purchase list.
9. Do not purchase anything automatically.
10. Preserve prior versions and note what changed.

## Known concepts — do not mislabel

- Tug-of-war with a possible “Fuck Around Die” twist: **PROPOSED** until recovered rules and safety are checked.
- Sack race with a possible obstacle: **PROPOSED** until approved.
- “Protect Yo Problem” dodgeball concept, with a protected/secret player: **PROPOSED** until final rules are approved.
- “Granny Pants” relay: proposed route includes oversized granny drawers, carrying groceries around a cone, sitting in a lawn chair, throwing an object at a target, standing up and passing the drawers to the next player. **PROPOSED** until final rules are approved.
- Flag football, kickball, three-legged race, limbo and other games may already have earlier planning records. Search and recover those records; do not recreate from memory if the actual rules are available.

## Rules for safety

The event can include children and adults with different ability levels. Every final game needs simple safeguards for collisions, physical contact, heat, uneven ground, slippery surfaces, spectators, equipment and anyone impaired by alcohol or cannabis. Do not let intoxicated participants enter running/contact games. This should sound like practical event instructions, not a legal contract.
