# Operating Rules

## 1. Voice and articulation

The game book must sound like the event actually sounds.

Use:
- plain English;
- short sentences;
- direct commands;
- urban humor;
- shit-talking where it helps the personality;
- language people can understand while standing outside with music playing and people yelling.

Do **not** write like a corporate recreation handbook.

Bad:
> Participants will proceed toward the designated marker and execute the assigned task.

Better:
> Grab the bag, get around the cone, hit the target, and haul ass back. If you drop your shit, pick it up and keep moving.

The humor cannot make the rules confusing. Every game still needs an obvious answer to:
- Who goes?
- What do they do?
- What counts?
- What does not count?
- Who wins?
- What happens if somebody cheats, falls, misses, or argues?

## 2. Blackout Krew

Blackout Krew is the event's operating crew, not a competitive team.

Their role can include:
- refereeing;
- scoring;
- setup;
- cleanup;
- equipment staging;
- watching lines/boundaries;
- triggering approved twists;
- running the Fuck Around Die;
- calling fouls;
- breaking ties under the approved rule;
- keeping the next game ready;
- crowd/control support;
- cooking/serving/setup support where assigned.

Personality:
> Blackout Krew is the government of Rival Day.

Competitors can joke that Blackout is cheating. Blackout still owns the official call.

## 3. Competitive fairness

The team draw/assignment system was designed around **balanced randomness**, not careless pure randomness.

Rules to preserve:
- explicitly identified males/boys should be spread across teams as evenly as mathematically possible;
- do not infer male from a person's name;
- under the owner's standing rule, a person not explicitly marked male/boy is counted female unless later corrected;
- team sizes should stay as even as possible;
- households should be split where practical rather than stacked together;
- randomness only chooses among assignments that satisfy the fairness constraints;
- Blackout Krew is outside competitive balancing;
- practice/test draws are not official assignments;
- latest owner correction wins.

Why this matters to the Game Book:
physical games like tug-of-war, relays and carrying games should not be decided because one team accidentally received most of the adult males.

## 4. Game design rule

The equipment can be normal. The rules should be horribly unorthodox.

Use familiar equipment:
- footballs;
- kickballs;
- dodgeballs;
- tug rope;
- sacks;
- cones;
- cups;
- chairs;
- buckets;
- balloons;
- relay gear.

Then twist the rules so the game feels like Rival Day instead of school Field Day.

## 5. Safety rule

Every game card requires a safety section.

The game can be ridiculous without being reckless.

Examples of the kind of line that belongs here:
- no intentional headshots;
- no dangerous tackles unless a clearly safe contact rule is approved;
- stop play if somebody is down;
- wet-ground hazards must be watched;
- a Fuck Around Die result cannot introduce a dangerous condition;
- children/adults may need separate lanes or adjusted equipment depending on the final game.

Do not bury safety in legal language. Keep it direct.

## 6. Pressure without humiliation

Games should create clutch moments:
- everybody watching;
- phones out;
- teams heckling;
- one person having to perform under pressure;
- score tied;
- Blackout with the whistle.

The desired feeling:
> “I cannot let these motherfuckers clown me in the group tonight.”

The goal is competitive pressure, not humiliation.

## 7. Household wildcard rule

Each household can bring **one twisted/nontraditional game**.

It should be submitted privately so:
- duplicates can be prevented;
- surprise is preserved;
- Blackout can review equipment and safety;
- the game can be scheduled without everybody knowing it in advance.

The private master list should record:
- household;
- game name;
- equipment;
- estimated duration;
- safety concerns;
- duplicate status;
- owner approval;
- Blackout approval to run.

## 8. Scoring status

A unified final scoring table was **not fully locked** in the recovered work.

Known requirements:
- Blackout maintains the official score;
- the War Board/scoreboard reflects game results;
- ties need an explicit approved tiebreak;
- rules cannot be changed after a completed result;
- any Fuck Around Die mutation must be declared during the round, not retroactively.

Do not invent a final universal point system and label it approved until the owner approves it.

## 9. Time control

Every game card needs an estimated duration.

The book should support scheduling the whole day and prevent one game from accidentally eating 45 minutes.

Suggested status labels are fine for planning, but exact timing remains game-specific:
- Quick
- Standard
- Main Event

## 10. Reusability

The book should work next year too.

Use:
- reusable equipment;
- swappable twists;
- mutation tables;
- household wildcards;
- game cards that can be updated without rewriting the entire book.
